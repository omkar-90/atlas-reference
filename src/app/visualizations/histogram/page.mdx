# Histogram, Statistical Tolerances & Distribution Analysis

The **Histogram** module provides empirical probability density estimation, shape characterization, and statistical tolerance boundary inspection ($\pm 1\sigma, \pm 3\sigma$). In engineering design, analyzing the dispersion, skewness, and tails of input factors and responses ensures that surrogate models do not extrapolate across sparsely sampled regions.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick Guide & Key Takeaways">
- **Under-Sampled Tail Warning**: Sparse bins in the tails ($|x - \mu| > 2.5\sigma$) indicate regions where surrogate model predictive uncertainty will be highest.
- **Multimodal Distribution Warning**: Bimodal or multimodal distributions suggest mixed operational regimes or physical bifurcations (e.g., subcritical vs. supercritical flows).
- **Tolerance Limits ($\pm 3\sigma$)**: Six-sigma quality checks compare empirical output spreads against Upper and Lower Specification Limits ($\text{USL}$ and $\text{LSL}$).
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to Read This Plot

### 1. Axes & Visual Elements
- **Horizontal Axis (X)**: Value range of the selected parameter or response.
- **Vertical Axis (Y)**: Frequency count (number of samples falling in each bin) or probability density.
- **Histogram Bars**: Group data into equal-width bins to show empirical frequency.
- **KDE Curve (Blue Line)**: Continuous estimate of the underlying probability density function.

### 2. Reading Central Tendency & Dispersion
- **Mean (Red Dashed Line)**: Arithmetic average of the dataset.
- **$\pm 1\sigma$ Band (Inner Region)**: Encompasses $\approx 68.3\%$ of the samples under near-normal conditions.
- **$\pm 3\sigma$ Band (Outer Region)**: Encompasses $\approx 99.7\%$ of expected operational variance.

### 3. Key Diagnostic Patterns
- **Symmetric Bell Shape**: Data is normally distributed; suitable for linear or standard Gaussian process surrogate modeling without scaling transforms.
- **Right / Positive Skew (Mean > Median)**: Long tail stretching to the right (e.g. pressure spikes or pollutant concentrations). Suggests applying a logarithmic transformation ($\log(y)$) prior to fitting polynomial models.
- **Bimodal Peaks**: Two distinct crests indicate that samples originate from two different operational regimes (e.g. laminar vs. turbulent flow). The dataset should be partitioned or trained with a classification gating model.
- **Empty Bins within the Domain**: Point out sampling voids where surrogate predictions will have wide confidence intervals.

## Mathematical Formulation

### Optimal Bin Width (Freedman-Diaconis Rule)

To avoid subjective bin choices, bin width $h$ is computed using the interquartile range:

$$h = 2 \cdot \operatorname{IQR}(\mathbf{x}) \cdot N^{-1/3}$$

where $\operatorname{IQR}(\mathbf{x}) = Q_3(\mathbf{x}) - Q_1(\mathbf{x})$ and $N$ is the sample size.

### Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)

$$\hat{f}_h(x) = \frac{1}{N h} \sum_{i=1}^N K\left( \frac{x - x_i}{h} \right)$$

where $K(u) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-u^2/2}$ is the standard Gaussian kernel.
