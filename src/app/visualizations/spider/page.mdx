# Spider & Radar Charts: Multi-Criteria Profile Benchmarking

The **Spider / Radar Chart** (also known as a Kiviat diagram or star plot) provides a radial, equiangular visualization for benchmarking discrete design variants against baseline performance targets across multiple criteria simultaneously. In multi-attribute decision making, radar charts allow users to compare complete design signatures at a glance and ensure balanced system performance.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick Guide & Key Takeaways">
- **Design Signature Comparison**: Superimposing a candidate design polygon against a baseline envelope immediately reveals where performance is gained or compromised.
- **Axis Permutation Awareness**: The enclosed polygonal area depends on the ordering of the axes; related physical criteria (e.g. structural, aerodynamic, thermal) should be grouped together.
- **Normalized Dimensions**: All metrics are rescaled to a common $[0, 1]$ interval, preventing large-magnitude metrics (e.g. Pressure in Pa) from masking fractional metrics (e.g. Drag Coefficient $C_D$).
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to Read This Plot

### 1. Axes & Coordinates
- **Concentric Webs**: Represent normalized score rings, expanding from $0.0$ at the center hub to $1.0$ at the outermost perimeter.
- **Radial Spokes**: Each spoke represents an evaluation criterion. Spoke vertices are positioned so that larger radius ($r \to 1.0$) corresponds to better or compliant performance.
- **Polygonal Footprint**: Each design variant forms a connected loop across all spokes.

### 2. Comparative Analysis (Candidate vs. Baseline)
- **Outward Bulges**: In Figure 1, the candidate design expands outward on **Efficiency** and **Thermal Margin**, indicating substantial improvements over the baseline.
- **Inward Retreats**: Look for any vertices retracting toward the center. If a design retreats inside the baseline polygon on any critical spoke, that design suffers from a performance penalty.
- **Balanced vs. Skewed Profiles**: A symmetrical, balanced polygon indicates robust multidisciplinary performance, whereas a spiked, asymmetrical shape indicates a design over-specialized on one metric at the expense of others.

## Mathematical Formulation

Let $K$ be the number of normalized criteria. In a spider plot, $K$ equiangular radial axes radiate from a common origin:

$$\theta_k = \frac{2\pi (k - 1)}{K}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$$

### Boundary Normalization

Given metric value $z_k$ for a candidate design, its radial coordinate $r_k \in [0, 1]$ is:

$$r_k = \frac{z_k - z_k^{\min}}{z_k^{\max} - z_k^{\min}}$$

Cartesian coordinates $(u_k, v_k)$ are obtained via polar transformation:

$$u_k = r_k \cos(\theta_k), \qquad v_k = r_k \sin(\theta_k)$$

### Enclosed Polygon Area

The area $A$ enclosed by the polygon connecting vertices $\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_K, \mathbf{v}_1$ is computed using the cross-product formula:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{K}\right) \sum_{k=1}^K r_k r_{k+1}, \quad \text{with } r_{K+1} \equiv r_1$$
