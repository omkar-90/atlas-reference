# Anthill Plot: Multi-Dimensional 2D/3D Scatter Diagnostics

The **Anthill Plot** is a high-density multi-dimensional scatter visualization used to evaluate raw dataset distributions across 2D or 3D coordinate spaces. Operating directly on the uploaded experimental or simulation dataset without requiring prior model training, the Anthill plot projects design points as a scatter swarm to uncover design-space coverage, identify point clusters, spot anomalous outliers, and visualize complex nonlinear physical relationships.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick Guide & Key Takeaways">
- **Design-Space Coverage**: Verify that sample points cover the design space uniformly without large empty voids or unintended clustering.
- **Visualizing Up to 4 Dimensions**: Plot variables on the X, Y, and optional Z axes, while mapping a 4th parameter to a continuous marker color scale.
- **Nonlinear & Monotonic Trends**: Detect quadratic curvature, exponential growth, saturation plateaus, and discontinuities between any pair of variables.
- **Outlier Diagnostics**: Spot isolated solver evaluations that sit far from the main sample distribution due to meshing issues or solver divergence.
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to Read This Plot

### 1. Axes & Coordinate Selection
- **X Axis and Y Axis**: Select any two continuous or discrete variables from your dataset (input parameters or output responses).
- **Z Axis (3D Mode)**: When 3D mode is toggled, a third spatial axis is added, allowing 360-degree interactive rotation and depth inspection.
- **Colour By (4th Dimension)**: Mapping an additional variable to marker color projects a continuous colorbar (such as Viridis), enabling simultaneous evaluation of up to 4 dimensions ($X, Y, Z, \text{Color}$).

### 2. What to Look For in the Scatter Swarm
- **Uniformity & Space-Filling**: In an ideal Design of Experiments (such as Latin Hypercube Sampling or Sobol sequences), points should be distributed evenly across the coordinate space.
- **Sparse Voids**: Large empty gaps within the expected parameter range $[x_{\min}, x_{\max}]$ indicate under-sampled regions where surrogate prediction variance will be high.
- **Nonlinear Physical Trends**: Curved bands (such as the quadratic trend in Figure 1) show direct relationships between parameters. For instance, increasing Blade Angle causes a curved increase in Peak Stress.
- **Color Progression**: Observe whether point colors transition smoothly along the trend. A smooth color gradient confirms that the third variable acts as a co-driver of the response.

### 3. Spotting Outliers and Anomalies
- **Isolated Points (Red Marker)**: Points that sit far away from the main swarm often represent simulation solver divergence, invalid boundary condition inputs, or numerical mesh singularities.
- **Point Size and Opacity Sliders**: When analyzing large datasets ($N > 2{,}000$ points), individual dots can overlap and obscure internal structure. Lowering the **Point Opacity** slider and reducing **Point Size** reveals density concentrations and internal clusters within the swarm.

## Mathematical Formulation

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{\mathbf{z}_i\}_{i=1}^N$ be the dataset of evaluated configurations, where each observation $\mathbf{z}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d+p}$ contains all input parameters and output responses.

### Cartesian Coordinate Projection

The Anthill plot projects observations onto selected coordinate dimensions $(u, v)$:

$$\mathcal{P}_{uv}: \mathbb{R}^{d+p} \to \mathbb{R}^2, \quad \mathbf{z}_i \mapsto \big(z_{iu}, z_{iv}\big)$$

When 3D mode is active, the projection maps to $\mathbb{R}^3$:

$$\mathcal{P}_{uvw}: \mathbb{R}^{d+p} \to \mathbb{R}^3, \quad \mathbf{z}_i \mapsto \big(z_{iu}, z_{iv}, z_{iw}\big)$$

### Color Mapping Formulation

When a color column $c$ is assigned, marker color $C_i$ is mapped through a continuous colormap function $\mathcal{M}: [0, 1] \to \text{RGB}$:

$$C_i = \mathcal{M}\left( \frac{z_{ic} - z_c^{\min}}{z_c^{\max} - z_c^{\min}} \right)$$

This allows immediate visual detection of three-way interactions: $y = f(x_1, x_2)$.
