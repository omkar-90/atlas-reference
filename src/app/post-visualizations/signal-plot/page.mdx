# Signal & Field Plot: Functional Data Analysis (FDA)

The **Signal / Field Plot** module extends surrogate modeling from discrete scalar metrics ($y \in \mathbb{R}$) to continuous physical curves ($y(t) \in \mathcal{L}^2(\mathcal{T})$) and spatial distributions. In transient dynamic simulations—such as vehicle crash pulses over time, combustion pressure cycles, aerodynamic airfoil distributions, or frequency response spectra—performance is evaluated as a continuous curve. This module applies **Functional Data Analysis (FDA)** to decompose, model, and predict complete functional trajectory envelopes.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick Guide & Key Takeaways">
- **Continuous Curve Surrogates**: Rather than fitting separate scalar models at arbitrary time slices, FDA models the entire continuous physical trajectory simultaneously.
- **Variability Envelopes**: Displays the functional mean curve $\mu(t)$ flanked by confidence intervals ($\pm 1\sigma(t), \pm 2\sigma(t)$), pinpointing time windows of maximum physical variation.
- **Phase vs. Amplitude Shifts**: Distinguishes whether curve variation arises from horizontal time shifts (e.g., ignition delays) or vertical amplitude scaling (e.g., shock magnitude).
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to Read This Plot

### 1. Axes & Coordinates
- **Horizontal Axis (t)**: The continuous independent domain (e.g., time in milliseconds, angle in degrees, or normalized spatial span $x/L$).
- **Vertical Axis ($Y(t)$)**: The functional response quantity (e.g., cylinder pressure, structural reaction force, or aerodynamic lift coefficient).

### 2. Reading Trajectory Envelopes
- **Mean Curve $\mu(t)$ (Thick Line)**: The central baseline trajectory averaged across all design points.
- **Variability Band ($\pm 1\sigma(t)$)**: A time-varying confidence envelope. Where the band is wide, input parameters exert strong control over the output; where the band pinches narrow, the response is physically constrained or insensitive to design changes.
- **Peak Variance Window (Red Marker)**: Points out the exact time coordinate where design choices cause the greatest divergence in physical performance.

### 3. Interpreting Functional Dynamics
- **Amplitude Variation**: The curves peak at the same time coordinate ($t \approx 190\text{ ms}$), but vary in vertical height. This indicates that inputs control peak magnitude without altering dynamic frequency.
- **Phase Variation (Timing Delays)**: If curves shift horizontally left and right, inputs control reaction speed, ignition delay, or natural frequency.

## Mathematical Formulation

In Functional Data Analysis (Ramsay & Silverman, 2005), discrete sampled signals $\mathbf{y}_i = [y_{i1}, \dots, y_{iM}]^T$ are converted into continuous functions $x_i(t)$ using a linear combination of $K$ basis functions:

$$x_i(t) = \sum_{k=1}^K c_{ik} \phi_k(t) = \mathbf{c}_i^T \boldsymbol{\phi}(t)$$

### Basis Selection
- **B-Splines**: Optimal for non-periodic transient dynamics (e.g. crash pulses, force-displacement).
- **Fourier Basis**: Optimal for cyclic, periodic signals (e.g. rotating vibration spectra).

### Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA)

Surrogate models for continuous curves are constructed via the **Karhunen-Loève Expansion**:

$$x_i(t) = \mu(t) + \sum_{k=1}^\infty \xi_{ik} \psi_k(t)$$

where $\mu(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N x_i(t)$ is the functional mean, $\psi_k(t)$ are orthogonal eigenfunctions, and $\xi_{ik}$ are scalar principal component scores.

Standard Gaussian Process or Neural Network models are trained on the scalar scores $\xi_1(\mathbf{x}), \dots, \xi_K(\mathbf{x})$. When querying an unseen design $\mathbf{x}^*$, the predicted scores are converted back into a full continuous curve:

$$\hat{y}(\mathbf{x}^*, t) = \mu(t) + \sum_{k=1}^K \hat{\xi}_k(\mathbf{x}^*) \psi_k(t)$$
