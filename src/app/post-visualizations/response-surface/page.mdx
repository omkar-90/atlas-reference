# Response Surface Methodology (RSM) & Surrogate Landscapes

The **Response Surface** module renders continuous 3D topographical landscapes and 2D contour projections of trained surrogate metamodels (such as Kriging / Gaussian Process Regression, Support Vector Machines, Radial Basis Functions, or Neural Networks). In engineering optimization, response surfaces replace expensive, hours-long simulation runs with real-time continuous models, enabling rapid exploration of performance peaks, valleys, and saddle points.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick Guide & Key Takeaways">
- **Hyperplane Slicing**: When evaluating systems with $d > 2$ input parameters, the remaining $(d-2)$ variables are held fixed at designated baseline values (typically the median or current best design).
- **Curvature & Non-Linearity**: Valleys, ridges, and saddle shapes reveal coupled interaction terms between the selected parameter pair.
- **Uncertainty Bounds**: In Kriging models, prediction variance $s^2(\mathbf{x})$ confirms that model confidence is highest near dense training points and lower in un-sampled voids.
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to Read This Plot

### 1. Axes & Coordinates
- **Base Grid ($X_1$ and $X_2$)**: Two continuous input parameters selected for visualization.
- **Vertical Axis ($Z$ / Elevation)**: Predicted surrogate response $\hat{y}(x_1, x_2)$ (e.g. Efficiency or Maximum Pressure).
- **Contour Rings**: Isoline contours connecting points of identical performance, similar to a topographic elevation map.

### 2. Identifying Surface Topology
- **Peaks (Domes)**: Regions of local or global maximum. In efficiency maximization or power yield problems, the highest crest identifies the optimum design point.
- **Valleys (Basins)**: Regions of local or global minimum. Useful when minimizing mass, aerodynamic drag, or mechanical stress.
- **Ridges & Ravines**: Elongated diagonal hills or valleys indicating that multiple combinations of $x_1$ and $x_2$ yield similar performance.
- **Saddle Points**: Points that are a maximum along one cross-section and a minimum along another. These require multi-directional gradient exploration.

### 3. Interpreting Slices in High-Dimensional Problems
- If the dataset has more than two inputs ($d > 2$), the 3D surface represents a **2D slice** through high-dimensional space.
- The remaining $(d-2)$ variables are held constant at fixed baseline values (displayed in the top-right indicator).
- When tuning parameters, shift the slice sliders to observe whether the peak shifts location, which indicates higher-order coupling between active and sliced parameters.

## Mathematical Formulation

Under Gaussian Process Regression (Kriging), the deterministic simulation response $y(\mathbf{x})$ is modeled as a trend plus a stationary Gaussian random process:

$$Y(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})^T \boldsymbol{\beta} + Z(\mathbf{x})$$

where $Z(\mathbf{x})$ has spatial covariance $\operatorname{Cov}\big(Z(\mathbf{x}), Z(\mathbf{x}')\big) = \sigma^2 R(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}', \boldsymbol{\theta})$.

### Best Linear Unbiased Predictor (BLUP)

Given training observations $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, the surrogate prediction at any query point $\mathbf{x}^*$ is:

$$\hat{y}(\mathbf{x}^*) = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}^*)^T \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} + \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}^*)^T \mathbf{R}^{-1} \left( \mathbf{y} - \mathbf{F} \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \right)$$

where $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}^*)$ is the cross-correlation vector and $\mathbf{R}$ is the correlation matrix across evaluated points.

### Prediction Variance

The epistemic uncertainty at query location $\mathbf{x}^*$ is:

$$s^2(\mathbf{x}^*) = \sigma^2 \left[ 1 - \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}^*)^T \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}^*) + \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}^*)^T \left( \mathbf{F}^T \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{F} \right)^{-1} \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}^*) \right]$$

Near training points, $s^2(\mathbf{x}^*) \to 0$, while in empty design space voids, $s^2(\mathbf{x}^*)$ expands, signaling where additional simulation runs should be placed.
