# Residual Diagnostics & Goodness-of-Fit Verification

The **Residual Diagnostics** module evaluates the accuracy, unbiasedness, and homoscedasticity of trained surrogate models. While summary scalar metrics (such as $R^2$ and $\text{RMSE}$) provide an aggregate score, residual plots reveal localized modeling deficiencies, missing non-linear terms, non-constant variance (heteroscedasticity), and solver convergence outliers.

<KeyTakeaways title="Quick Guide & Key Takeaways">
- **Predicted vs. Actual Alignment**: High-fidelity surrogate models place points tightly along the $45^\circ$ identity line ($y = \hat{y}$). Systematic deviation at extrema indicates regression shrinkage or under-prediction of physical peaks.
- **Funnel / Fan Patterns**: Residuals expanding outwards as predicted values increase signal **heteroscedasticity** (variance grows with response magnitude), indicating that a logarithmic or Box-Cox transformation is necessary.
- **Curvature in Residuals**: U-shaped or parabolic patterns in residuals indicate that the model omitted an important higher-order polynomial interaction.
</KeyTakeaways>

## How to Read This Plot

### 1. Predicted vs. Actual (Left Panel)
- **The $45^\circ$ Target Line (Green)**: Represents exact equality between surrogate predictions and simulation truth ($y = \hat{y}$).
- **Tight Point Swarm**: A narrow band hugging the $45^\circ$ line indicates high goodness-of-fit and minimal variance.
- **Extrema Flattens**: If points drift horizontally at the high or low ends, the surrogate is under-predicting physical peaks (regression to the mean).

### 2. Residuals vs. Predicted (Right Panel)
- **Horizontal Zero Line**: Residual errors $e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$ should fluctuate symmetrically around zero without systematic bias.
- **Random Constant-Width Band**: An even, rectangular scatter of points across all predicted values confirms **homoscedasticity** (constant error variance).
- **Outlier Threshold ($\pm 3\sigma$)**: Any sample landing outside the red dashed boundaries is a studentized outlier ($|r_i| > 3$) and should be investigated in simulation logs.

### 3. Diagnostic Warning Patterns
- **Funnel / Megaphone Shape**: Errors widening as $\hat{y}$ increases indicates heteroscedastic variance. Common in fracture mechanics and fluid dynamics where absolute variance scales with response magnitude.
- **Curved Bow Shape (U or $\cap$)**: A parabolic curve in the residual points indicates that a quadratic or interaction term was missed by the surrogate.
- **Non-Zero Median**: If the majority of points sit on one side of zero, the surrogate has a constant offset bias.

## Mathematical Formulation

### Raw and Studentized Residuals

Given actual evaluation $y_i$ and surrogate prediction $\hat{y}_i$, the raw residual is:

$$e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$$

Because raw residuals retain physical units and depend on parameter leverage, the **Studentized Residual** ($r_i$) is used for outlier screening:

$$r_i = \frac{e_i}{\hat{\sigma} \sqrt{1 - h_{ii}}}$$

where $\hat{\sigma} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N - p - 1} \sum_{i=1}^N e_i^2}$ is the residual standard deviation and $h_{ii}$ is the leverage diagonal of the projection matrix $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}^T$.

### Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$) and Adjusted $R^2$

$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \bar{y})^2}, \qquad R_{\text{adj}}^2 = 1 - \left[ \frac{(1 - R^2)(N - 1)}{N - k - 1} \right]$$

### Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)

$$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 }$$
